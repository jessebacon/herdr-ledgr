## 1. Systems and papers: interaction model, context per branch, merging, evaluation

### Takeaway
There are two families. (a) Content-structuring canvases that turn LLM output into nodes, graphs or hierarchies for sensemaking (Sensecape, Graphologue, Luminate, Mindalogue, MindTrellis). (b) Conversation-structuring interfaces that make the chat itself a tree or graph (ChatGPTree/ChatGraPhT, Canvas Chat, CanvasConvo, Contextify, Branchat, Conversation Tree Architecture). Context handling is usually "ancestor path only"; merging is the least-solved feature and most chat-branching systems have none. Controlled comparisons against linear chat are few, small, and mixed.

### Cited Findings

#### Family A: Content canvases, graphs, hierarchies (sensemaking)

**Sensecape** — Suh, Min, Palani, Xia (UCSD). UIST 2023 **[PR]**. [arXiv 2305.11483](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11483), DOI 10.1145/3586183.3606756.
- Model: infinite canvas (nodes, grouping, edges) plus a "hierarchy view" that externalizes levels of abstraction; each subtopic gets its own canvas; designed to switch between foraging and sensemaking — [paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.11483).
- Per-node actions via an "expand bar": Prompt, Explain, Questions, Subtopics; semantic zoom shows Lines / Summary / Keywords versions of a node; users extract parts of a response into nodes — [paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.11483).
- Context/merge: the canvas topic label is LLM-summarized from canvas content and updated as nodes are added; subtopic generation takes a `{context}` string in its prompt; no merge-of-branches operation (the structure is a hierarchy that users build upward/downward) — [paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.11483).
- Study: within-subject, N=12, vs a Baseline of chat + basic concept-map canvas (not plain ChatGPT; the authors dropped an earlier weaker baseline after a pilot). Prompts issued similar (5.8 vs 7.3, p=.37); Sensecape users explored more concepts (68.3 vs 22.8, p=.01), built more hierarchy levels (4.3 vs 2.6, p=.02), and revisited prior information more (12.8 vs 0.7, p<.01). Seven of 12 preferred Sensecape overall; 9 preferred it for deeper understanding; for a broad overview 6 preferred Baseline, 5 Sensecape, 1 neither. Baseline was rated marginally easier to navigate. No NASA-TLX; measures were counts plus survey/interview — [paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.11483).
- Negatives: the hierarchy view was "complex and overwhelming" for a few participants; learning curve was the main reason for preferring Baseline; ~20-minute sessions; novelty effect acknowledged; authors suggest offering a switch back to a linear view — [paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.11483).
- Code/demo: none found (searched project page and code; the paper says it is a React web app built on Reactflow) — [search result](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.11483).

**Graphologue** — Peiling Jiang, Jude Rayan, Steven P. Dow, Haijun Xia (UCSD). UIST 2023 **[PR]**. [arXiv 2305.11473](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11473).
- Model: GPT-4 responses are annotated in-stream and converted into node-link diagrams in real time; users click nodes/edges to issue follow-up prompts (explain, examples), hover to co-highlight source text, and toggle between split (per-paragraph) and merged diagrams, original text, outline and summary views — [paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.11473).
- Context/merge: diagrams are per response paragraph; users can merge diagrams and merge nodes by dragging one onto another; summaries are generated per paragraph to keep context when switching complexity levels. Authors list "summarize a branch of a diagram with a higher-level concept" as future work — [paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.11473).
- Study: formative study N=10 with ChatGPT (verbose, unstructured responses; users wanted to organize info across back-and-forth). Evaluation N=7 experienced ChatGPT users, ~20 min task + survey/interview, **no baseline condition**, 5-point Likert plus thematic analysis; positive on comprehension and control of diagram complexity — [paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.11473).
- Negatives: lots of scrolling when diagrams are split; diagrams confusing when they did not match the user's mental model or had too much detail ("a little intimidating", P5); animations distracting; annotation errors; participants chose topics they were familiar with — [paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.11473).
- Code/demo: none found.

**Luminate** — Sangho Suh, Meng Chen, Bryan Min, Toby Jia-Jun Li, Haijun Xia. CHI 2024 **[PR]**. [arXiv 2310.12953](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12953), DOI 10.1145/3613904.3642400.
- Model: LLM proposes ~5 categorical "dimensions" with values; generates responses spread across the dimension-value space; responses shown as dots on a canvas that rearrange into 1D/2D scatter plots; five semantic-zoom levels (dot, title, keyword, summary, full text); "more like this"/"add more"; bookmarks and filters — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.12953).
- Context: full editor text passed as background; highlighted text added as focused context. No branch tree — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.12953).
- Study: N=14 professional writers (Upwork), 80 min, 25-min task; Creativity Support Index 82.16 (SD 5.14), Exploration highest; **no baseline**, comparison to ChatGPT only qualitative; 10/14 preferred Luminate for creative writing, one preferred ChatGPT for simplicity — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.12953).
- Negatives: several found 40 responses overwhelming; five gave neutral/negative ease-of-use ratings citing short learning time — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.12953).
- Code/demo: yes. Live system and GitHub (project-luminate/luminate) linked from the [project page](https://luminate-research.github.io/).

**Mindalogue** — Rui Zhang, Ziyao Zhang, Fengliang Zhu, Jiajie Zhou, Anyi Rao. arXiv Oct 2024; the PDF uses the CHI 2025 template but I found no confirmation of acceptance, so treat as **[PP]**. [arXiv 2410.10570](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10570).
- Model: "nodes + canvas" mind map; initial 4-layer map; per-node Explanation, Examples, Exploration actions; collapse/expand, drag, zoom — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10570v1).
- Context: exploring a node passes the **parent node's** information as context (the page does not say the full ancestor chain is passed). No merge or summary mechanism described — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10570v1).
- Study: formative N=11; evaluation N=16, ~120 min, four tasks (two unfamiliar, two familiar), vs a linear baseline "Mindmap Master" on GPT-4o; measures: time, actions, task questions (incl. a CSI item), 9-item survey (SUS/NASA-TLX items), interviews — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10570v1).
- Results (as reported in the body): Mindalogue took **longer** (10.16 vs 7.78 min) and **more** actions (11.19 vs 6.81) than the linear baseline; task-support ratings were slightly higher for Mindalogue on all four tasks; the linear system was easier to learn (learning-effort item favored baseline, p=.008) — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10570v1).
- **Conflict**: the abstract says Mindalogue "significantly reduced task steps and improved users' comprehension" — [abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10570) — while the body reports more actions and time for Mindalogue — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10570v1). The body numbers should be treated as the better evidence.
- Negatives: participants "easily lost direction" in complex node networks; higher learning curve; repetition/redundancy on expansion — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10570v1).
- Code/demo: none seen.

**MindTrellis** — Xiang Li, Cara Li, Emily Kuang, Can Liu, Jian Zhao. arXiv Apr 2026 **[PP]**. [arXiv 2604.23129](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.23129).
- Model: user and AI co-build a dynamic knowledge graph from multiple documents; users query the graph, add concepts, edit relations, reorganize hierarchy. Study: N=12 creating slide decks vs retrieval-only baselines; better knowledge organization and cognitive load per abstract; no numbers visible (abstract only) — [abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.23129).

**Thinkink** — Payandeh, Vogel, Zhao. arXiv Jul 2026 **[PP]**. [arXiv 2607.21468](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21468). Handwritten/sketched prompts on a shared 2D canvas; LLM replies rendered as ink beside the user's marks; formative N=12, diagnostic N=6, final N=10; no results in the abstract.

**Selenite** — Liu, Wu, Chen, Li, Kittur, Myers. CHI 2024 **[PR]**. [arXiv 2310.02161](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02161). LLM-elicited overviews of options/criteria for web sensemaking (overview, not branching); three studies reported improved comprehension and processing speed (per [search summary](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02161v4); abstract-level only).

**Threddy** — Kang, Chang, Kim, Kittur. UIST 2022 **[PR]**. [arXiv 2208.03455](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.03455). Not LLM-based; "research threads" organized while reading. Survey vs Google Docs: higher on finding more papers per thread (6.1 vs 4.2, p=.04) but did **not** reduce overall task demand or organizing effort, and adoption likelihood did not differ (authors suggest familiarity bias favoring Google Docs) — [search summary of appendix](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.03455).

**Synergi** — Kang, Wu, Chang, Kittur. UIST 2023 **[PR]**. [arXiv 2308.07517](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.07517). Seed research threads expanded using citation graphs plus GPT-4 hierarchical summaries; outline editor; compared with a baseline and with a ChatGPT-4-only system, producing better-supported threads (abstract/search-level) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.07517).

#### Family B: Conversation trees, graphs and branching chat

**ChatGPTree / ChatBraid / ChatGraPhT** — Geoff Kimm, Linus Tan (Swinburne). arXiv Dec 2025 **[PP]**. [arXiv 2512.22790](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.22790).
- Model: three prototypes. ChatGPTree = conversation as branching tree; ChatGraPhT = node-link canvas with Graph Agent + Meta Agent for reflection guidance, grounded in Schon's reflective practice — [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.22790).
- Context: "each branch is a distinct conversational context: the LLM receives only the dialogue history from a new user prompt back to the original conversational root" (ChatGPTree) — [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.22790).
- Merge: **yes** — a user-prompt node can have any number of parent LLM-response nodes ("synthesise divergent reflections into a cohesive dialogue"); downstream nodes auto-update — [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.22790).
- Evaluation: first-person / author-based inquiry only; no external user study (planned: TSRI, SUS, NASA-TLX) — [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.22790).

**Canvas Chat** — Eric J. Ma. Blog + open source, 31 Dec 2025 **[BL]**. [Post](https://ericmjl.github.io/blog/2025/12/31/canvas-chat-a-visual-interface-for-thinking-with-llms/), [GitHub](https://github.com/ericmjl/canvas-chat).
- Model: infinite canvas, nodes in a directed graph; reply on any node to branch; select text in a node and ask a follow-up to create a "highlight node" with the excerpt as blockquote — [post](https://ericmjl.github.io/blog/2025/12/31/canvas-chat-a-visual-interface-for-thinking-with-llms/).
- Context: walks the graph backward from the selected node(s), collects all ancestors sorted by creation time and sends them as history; for multi-select it sends the deduplicated union of ancestors — [post](https://ericmjl.github.io/blog/2025/12/31/canvas-chat-a-visual-interface-for-thinking-with-llms/).
- Merge: Cmd-click several nodes and send one message to synthesize across branches; matrix evaluation cells can be pinned as nodes — [post](https://ericmjl.github.io/blog/2025/12/31/canvas-chat-a-visual-interface-for-thinking-with-llms/). No evaluation.

**Contextify** ("Mixed-Initiative Context: Structuring and Managing Context for Human-AI Collaboration") — Haichang Li, Qinshi Zhang, Piaohong Wang, Zhicong Lu. arXiv Apr 2026 **[PP]**. [arXiv 2604.07121](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.07121).
- Model: chat + collapsible node-based Context Map; hover on message offers Branch, Context (toggle active), Edit; proactive AI suggests branching, returning, extracting patterns; four agents (Conversation, Structure, Memory, User Model) — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.07121).
- Context: mainline mode = all active units from origin to current node; sub-thread mode inherits mainline up to the branch anchor and isolates parallel paths; manual Include/Exclude of nodes; Memory Agent compresses information between mainline and branches — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.07121).
- Merge: no dedicated merge operation described (paths "reconnected when needed") — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.07121).
- Study: N=6 within-subjects vs ChatGPT linear chat, two 15-min hardware-design tasks; SUS 72.08; **no statistical comparison** with the baseline; authors say findings do not support generalizable claims — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.07121).
- Negatives: branching added cognitive load (P2); short/simple tasks did not justify the overhead (P3 just started a new chat); some forgot to branch without prompts; learning cost of the node representation — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.07121).

**CanvasConvo** ("Conversations in Space: Non-Linear LLM Interaction in Everyday Use", v1 title "Structuring Non-Linear LLM Interactions on a Canvas") — Rifat Mehreen Amin, Alperen Adatepe, Daniela Fernandes, Daniel Buschek, Andreas Butz (LMU Munich / Bayreuth). arXiv May 2026, v2 Sept 2026 **[PP]**. [arXiv 2605.15848](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15848).
- Model: chat view synchronized with a spatial canvas showing the conversation as a branching tree; branch from any message in either view; branch zones with auto-summaries, tags, timeline navigation, sticky-note annotations, text-selection transform toolbar, prompt templates — [HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.15848v2).
- Context: a branch duplicates history up to the branch point (inherits ancestor path); the paper does not say whether a branch can see siblings; participants were unsure what a branch "knew" — [HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.15848v2).
- Merge: **none**; "supported divergence but not merging" (future work) — [HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.15848v2).
- Study: N=24, five-day field deployment, mostly experienced LLM users; 165 conversations, 1,225 messages; 79 branches (M=3.29 per participant, max nesting depth 2); event shares chat 47.2%, canvas 28.3%; SUS 78.9; NASA-TLX overall 2.79 (no baseline condition, so not comparable to chat); participants moved between views rather than replacing chat; canvas used for overview, revisitation, alternatives — [HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.15848v2), [abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15848).
- Negatives: unclear branch context reduced confidence; chat habits persisted (one participant never adopted the canvas); view switching confusing; cross-branch comparison and merging difficult; adoption uneven — [HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.15848v2).
- Code/demo: not stated; tutorial video as supplementary material only.

**Branchat** — Jiaming Li, Peinuan Qin, Yi-Chieh Lee (NUS). CHI 2026 Extended Abstracts (CHI EA '26, Barcelona, April 2026) **[EA]**. [DOI 10.1145/3772363.3798792](https://doi.org/10.1145/3772363.3798792); venue per [Crossref](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1145/3772363.3798792).
- Model: tree-structured interface for revisiting long LLM conversations; synchronized tree beside the linear transcript (description of tree+chat from the CanvasConvo related-work section: [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.15848v2)); lets users control context scoping.
- Study (abstract only, via Semantic Scholar; full text blocked by ACM 403): within-subject N=12 vs linear chat; "preliminary results suggest faster task completion, lower cognitive load, and higher trust and self-efficacy" — [Semantic Scholar](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1145/3772363.3798792?fields=title,authors,venue,year,abstract).

**Conversation Tree Architecture** — Pranav Hemanth, Sampriti Saha. arXiv Mar 2026 **[PP]**. [arXiv 2603.21278](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21278).
- Model: conversation as tree of context-isolated nodes to avoid "logical context poisoning"; each node has its own context window; context flows downstream parent-to-child on branch creation and upstream on deletion; temporary "volatile nodes" must be selectively merged into the parent or discarded. Formalization + prototype web app, no user study — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21278).

**Context-Agent** — Hu, Guo, Liu, Yin, Wei. ACL 2026 (per arXiv comments) **[PR, NLP venue]**. [arXiv 2604.05552](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.05552). Models multi-turn history as a dynamic discourse tree, new NTM benchmark for non-linear dialogue; claims better task completion and token efficiency; no numbers on the abstract page; code said to be on GitHub.

**Node-tree vs chatbot for AI explainability** — Lifei Wang, Natalie Friedman, Chengchao Zhu, Zeshu Zhu, S. Joy Mountford. XHRI 2025 workshop at HRI 2025 **[EA]**; arXiv Oct 2025. [arXiv 2510.06457](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.06457). Hierarchical interactive nodes you can navigate, refine and follow-up from. N=20 business users, four task types. Chatbot good for linear, step-by-step queries; node-tree better for brainstorming, task performance, decision support and trust "by preserving context" (abstract; specific metrics not given on the page).

**Sood, Llano, McCormack — "Do Conversational Interfaces Limit Creativity? Exploring Visual Graph Systems for Creative Writing"**. arXiv Jul 2025 **[PP]**. [arXiv 2507.08260](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.08260).
- Model: node-based graph where users chain generative models and reusable templates. Study N=8 (convenience sample), counterbalanced vs a conversational baseline, planning-type writing tasks. Raw means: graph system worse; with one participant (P4, who could not use the graph UI) removed, graph system beat chat on every metric except ease of use, but "not statistically significant"; graph system "much more difficult to use"; some participants said it helped them visualize and think through the writing process — [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.08260).

**VisCanvas** — Ueno, Danzy, Jiang, Bryan (ASU). arXiv Jul 2026, v3 Sept 2026 **[PP]**. [arXiv 2607.21886](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21886).
- Model: node canvas for visualization authoring with Text Input, Visualization, Merge and Menu nodes (Modify, Duplicate, Merge, Suggest; edge menus Fill Blank and Branch); semantic zoom in three levels — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.21886).
- Context: no persistent chat session; each operation is a separate LLM call with graph context passed explicitly; Suggest uses analysis goals along the root-to-current path — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.21886).
- Merge: Merge Node combines two visualizations (layered view limited to two layers) — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.21886).
- Study: within-subjects N=20 vs VisChat (linear chat baseline with the same backend). Task ease 6.00 vs 6.05 (p=.89); NASA-TLX no significant difference on any dimension; VisCanvas significantly better for "pursuing multiple directions in parallel" (p=.01); merge/comparison benefit not significant (p=.07); open-ended analysis preference 5.35/7 toward VisCanvas (14/20 above midpoint) but targeted analysis split (8 VisCanvas, 10 VisChat, 2 neutral); participants produced Hybrid/Tree topologies more often in VisCanvas (9 vs 4; 5 vs 2) and Linear more often in VisChat (8 vs 1) — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.21886).
- Negatives: preference for chat for familiarity, lower overhead and single-chain work; navigation/orientation costs may grow as graphs grow; possible TLX ceiling effects; short sessions — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.21886).
- Code/demo: yes, GitHub svl-at-asu/VisCanvas and OSF osf.io/gsxhn per the paper — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.21886).

**RaivenTracks** — Hugie, Irger, Guo, Moreland, Pugmire, Klasky, Pfister. arXiv Aug 2026 **[PP]**. [arXiv 2608.14869](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14869). Branchable version tree of validated visualization specs (restoring a node recompiles an artifact instead of replaying dialogue); no merge mentioned; pilot N=3, no baseline.

**CognitiveCanvas** — Hussain, Rahman, Siddik. arXiv Oct 2026 **[PP]**. [arXiv 2610.23559](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23559). 2D spatial canvas over a graph of facts distilled from chat logs for developers; claims 92.2% token reduction; no study details on the abstract page. Not a branching chat UI; included as an example of canvas-as-memory.

#### Family C: Spatial / branching authoring and generation tools

**Spellburst** — Tyler Angert, Miroslav Ivan Suzara, Jenny Han, Christopher Lawrence Pondoc, Hariharan Subramonyam. UIST 2023 **[PR]**. [arXiv 2308.03921](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.03921), DOI 10.1145/3586183.3606719.
- Model: node canvas of sketch nodes and operator nodes (duplicate, merge, extract, diff); auto-layout shows the version history tree so users do not tidy the canvas (chosen because of cognitive-load concerns in the formative study) — [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.03921).
- Context: prompt composed from manually written code, AI code, previous prompts and operator-node data (so context is the node's connected sketch, not a transcript) — [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.03921).
- Merge: **LLM-powered semantic merge** of two sketches plus a `diff` operator that produces a natural-language summary of changes; unexpected results were common in merges and the authors say this trades user control for ease — [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.03921).
- Study: formative interviews N=11 (artists); evaluation N=10 expert artists, 75 min, within-subject vs their usual IDE + ChatGPT; NASA-TLX and PSSUQ reported for Spellburst (mental demand highest at 9.9, SD 4.8; PSSUQ overall 7.67 as stated); 73% liked the interface, 90% found it easy to learn; many preferred branching over linear ChatGPT (P9: "The ChatGPT version felt very linear"; P8: "explore way more ideas per minute"), one preferred ChatGPT for familiarity — [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.03921).
- Code/demo: project page with paper and video only; no code link — [project page](https://spellburstllm.github.io).

**Loom** — Janus ("socketteer"), building on Reynolds & McDonell's "Multiversal views on language models" (2021). Open-source tool **[BL / pre-window theory paper]**. [GitHub](https://github.com/socketteer/loom); theory: [arXiv 2102.06391](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.06391), IUI 2021 HAIGEN workshop ([CEUR](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-2903/IUI21WS-HAIGEN-11.pdf)).
- Model: tree of text nodes; generate N children per node; "block multiverse" view plots continuations; bookmarks; in-place editing; linear read mode; hotkeys to merge a node with its parent or children and to combine trees; per-node hidden memory; local models via llama.cpp — [README](https://github.com/socketteer/loom). Path-to-root context is plausible but the README summary did not state it, so I mark that unverified.
- No user study that I found.

**Cells, Generators, and Lenses** — Tae Soo Kim, Yoonjoo Lee, Minsuk Chang, Juho Kim. UIST 2023 **[PR]**. [Project page](https://llm-objects.kixlab.org), [KAIST record](https://dspace.kaist.ac.kr/handle/10203/315213).
- Model: object-oriented framework: Cells (input units), Generators (model settings), Lenses (output spaces such as list, gallery, 2D); in the story interface, cells are arranged in a tree so writers can build branching and parallel plotlines; users link cells to several generators to compare configurations — [project page](https://llm-objects.kixlab.org).
- Evaluation: the project page lists none; the KAIST repository abstract (via search) mentions three redesigned interfaces and a workshop with three designers — [search summary of KAIST record](https://dspace.kaist.ac.kr/handle/10203/315213). I did not read the full paper text.
- Code: yes, ReactJS library [github.com/kixlab/llm-ui-objects](https://github.com/kixlab/llm-ui-objects).

**ChainForge** — Ian Arawjo, Chelse Swoopes, Priyan Vaithilingam, Martin Wattenberg, Elena Glassman. CHI 2024 **[PR]**. [arXiv 2309.09128](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.09128), DOI 10.1145/3613904.3642016.
- Model: node-based flow for comparing responses across models and prompt variations (for prompt engineering / hypothesis testing, not chat). Open-source, per paper. In-lab usability study (age breakdown in the paper sums to 21 participants; my count, not an explicit N in the text I read), mean rating 4.19/5 (SD 0.66), 18 wanted to use again; interview study with 8 real-world users; three usage modes identified (opportunistic exploration, limited evaluation, iterative refinement) — [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.09128).

**DirectGPT** — Damien Masson, Sylvain Malacria, Géry Casiez, Daniel Vogel. CHI 2024 **[PR]**. [arXiv 2310.03691](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03691), DOI 10.1145/3613904.3642462.
- Model: direct-manipulation layer over ChatGPT (persistent output view, localized prompting, drag objects into prompt, prompts as toolbar tools, undo/redo). Not branching, but a direct critique of the linear transcript. N=12 within-subject vs ChatGPT replica: ~50% faster (56 s vs 1m57), prompts per task 1.90 vs 3.67, words per prompt 5.83 vs 19.98, SUS 92 vs 53, all preferred DirectGPT; code-editing closeness difference not significant — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.03691v2).
- Code/demo: yes, [GitHub m-damien/DirectGPT](https://github.com/m-damien/DirectGPT) (needs OpenAI key).

**AI-Instruments** — Riche, Offenwanger, Gmeiner, Brown, Romat, Pahud, Marquardt, Inkpen, Hinckley (Microsoft Research). CHI 2025 (arXiv says slated for CHI 2025) **[PR]**. [arXiv 2502.18736](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18736). Prompts as reusable direct-manipulation instruments; shows multiple interpretations of ambiguous intent; N=12, qualitative; says chat prompts return "long, linear text" that makes backtracking hard.

**OOPrompt** — Xu, Ma, Chen. EICS 2026 (per arXiv comments) **[PR]**. [arXiv 2604.19114](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.19114). Prompts as structured manipulable artifacts; formative probe N=20; validation study results not on the abstract page.

**LLMartini** — Shi, Yang, Wang, Yin, Li, Gao, Yu. arXiv Oct 2025 **[PP]**. [arXiv 2510.19252](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.19252). Compare/compose outputs across multiple LLMs (aligned segments, consensus auto-merged, differences color-coded). N=18 vs manual methods; "significantly outperformed" on time, cognitive load and satisfaction (abstract only).

**Memolet** — Ryan Yen, Jian Zhao (Waterloo). UIST 2024 **[PR]**. [Project page](https://ryanyen2.github.io/publications/memolet), DOI 10.1145/3654777.3676388. Reifies conversational memories as manipulable objects for reuse across chats; within-subject N=12 (per UW record; results not read).

**AI Chains / PromptChainer** — AI Chains: Tongshuang Wu, Michael Terry, Carrie Cai, CHI 2022 **[PR]**, [arXiv 2110.01691](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.01691); N=20, chaining improved result quality and perceived transparency/controllability; users compared alternative strategies by observing parallel downstream effects. PromptChainer: Wu, Jiang, Donsbach, Gray, Molina, Terry, Cai, CHI '22 Extended Abstracts **[EA]**, [arXiv 2203.06566](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.06566), case studies with four designers/developers.

**Jigsaw** — David Chuan-En Lin, Nikolas Martelaro. CHI 2024 **[PR]**. [arXiv 2310.08574](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.08574). Puzzle-piece metaphor for chaining foundation models into multimodal prototypes; interview N=10 for design goals; reported improved understanding of model capabilities and use as a canvas for exploration and documentation. Project site jigsaw.to (not fetched).

**Rambler** (CHI 2024 **[PR]**, [arXiv 2401.10838](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.10838); Lin et al.) and **CoPrompt** (CHI 2024 **[PR]**, [arXiv 2310.09235](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.09235); Feng, Yen, You, Fan, Zhao, Lu) are adjacent, not branching/spatial chat systems. Rambler: gist extraction and macro-revision of dictated text, N=12 vs speech-to-text + ChatGPT, more control, similar text quality. CoPrompt: referring/sharing/linking prompts in collaborative natural-language programming, N=12 — [Rambler search summary](https://research.google/pubs/rambler-supporting-writing-with-speech-via-llm-assisted-gist-manipulation/), [CoPrompt page](https://ryanyen2.github.io/publications/coprompt).

**ExploreLLM** — Xiao Ma et al. (Google). arXiv Dec 2023 **[PP]** (CHI venue not confirmed on the arXiv page). [arXiv 2312.00763](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00763). Argues text chatbots impose heavy cognitive load in exploratory tasks; adds a schema-like structure for planning plus GUI integration; qualitative user study (N not shown on abstract page).

#### Family D: Product and generative-UI adjacent

- **ChatGPT "Branch in new chat"** — OpenAI, announced 4 Sept 2025 **[BL/product]**; opens a new chat carrying the conversation up to the chosen message and leaves the original intact (I did not find the official OpenAI release note; consistent across press) — [TMCnet](https://blog.tmcnet.com/blog/rich-tehrani/?p=25101), [BGR](https://bgr.com/1959993/chatgpt-branch-conversations-new-feature-now-available/).
- **Generative Interfaces for Language Models** — Jiaqi Chen, Yanzhe Zhang, Yutong Zhang, Yijia Shao, Diyi Yang (Stanford/Georgia Tech). arXiv Aug 2025, accepted to ACL 2026 Findings **[PR]**. [arXiv 2508.19227](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.19227), code [SALT-NLP/GenUI](https://github.com/SALT-NLP/GenUI). LLM generates a task-specific UI instead of a chat reply; reports "up to a 72% improvement in human preference"; a secondary summary says chat stayed competitive for math-heavy or simple explanatory queries — [secondary summary](https://aitopics.org/doc/arxivorg:C27E88F9).
- **Google Research, "Generative UI: LLMs are Effective UI Generators"** — blog/paper, 18 Nov 2025 **[BL + company paper]**. Gemini 3 Pro with tools, system instructions and post-processing; rolled out as "dynamic view" in the Gemini app and in AI Mode; human-expert-made sites rated highest, generative UI close behind, strong gap to other output formats (when ignoring generation speed); can take a minute or more; PAGEN dataset — [Google Research blog](https://research.google/blog/generative-ui-a-rich-custom-visual-interactive-user-experience-for-any-prompt/). I did not read the paper itself.

### Inferences
- Systems that avoid a merge operation (CanvasConvo, Mindalogue, Contextify) report "cross-branch comparison/merging" as an unresolved pain point; systems that implement merge (Spellburst, ChatGraPhT, VisCanvas, Canvas Chat, Loom) do so with an LLM call over multiple parents, which makes the merge a generation step rather than a deterministic combine. That is consistent with Spellburst's finding of surprising merge outputs.
- Most chat-branching work is 2025-2026 preprints with small samples. The 2023 UIST/CHI papers (Sensecape, Graphologue, Luminate, Spellburst) are the peer-reviewed anchors, and they target sensemaking or creative production rather than general chat.
- "Branching chat" has moved from research into products (ChatGPT, September 2025), which probably explains the 2026 wave of papers positioning themselves around revisitation and context scoping (Branchat, CanvasConvo, Contextify).

### Gaps
- I found no CHI 2025 or UIST 2025 full paper specifically on conversation trees/graphs for LLM chat. The only CHI 2025 item surfaced was AI-Instruments. The ACM Digital Library returned HTTP 403, so my coverage of ACM-only venues (including CHI 2026 full papers, UIST 2025, IUI 2026) is limited to what arXiv, Crossref, Semantic Scholar and web search exposed. This is a search limitation, not proof of absence.
- "Tree of Thoughts"-style *interfaces* (as opposed to the prompting method), "Mosaic" generative-UI work, and a "hypertext conversation" paper: nothing found that matches. A Hugging Face "AI Collaborative Chat System" demo with parent-linked refinements surfaced but has no paper — [search result](https://spwotton-collab.static.hf.space/index.html).
- Full text not read for: Branchat (abstract only), Context-Agent, MindTrellis, Thinkink, OOPrompt, LLMartini, AI-Instruments, Memolet, Selenite, Synergi, Cells/Generators/Lenses (evaluation details), Jigsaw, Google Generative UI paper.
- Code/demo confirmed: Luminate, Cells/Generators/Lenses library, DirectGPT, Canvas Chat, VisCanvas, GenUI (Stanford), ChainForge (open-source per paper), Loom, Conversation Tree Architecture (hosted prototype app). Not found: Sensecape, Graphologue, Spellburst (video only), CanvasConvo, Contextify, Mindalogue, Branchat.

## 2. Theoretical and critical essays on chat and linearity

### Takeaway
Critiques split into (1) affordance and discoverability (Wattenberger, Appleton), (2) isolation of responses with no working buffer (Wattenberger), (3) chat as limited and slow for precise work (Litt), (4) chat as a poor information-keeping medium and the push toward direct manipulation of representations (Lee). Linearity per se is stated most directly by the HCI papers (Sensecape, Graphologue, Mindalogue, ChatGraPhT, AI-Instruments), less so by the essayists. I did not find primary writing by Matuschak or Victor on LLM chat linearity.

### Cited Findings
- **Amelia Wattenberger, "Why Chatbots Are Not the Future"** **[BL]**, ~May 2023 (Simon Willison linked it on 15 May 2023). [Original](https://wattenberger.com/thoughts/boo-chatbots), [Willison link post](https://simonwillison.net/2023/May/15/why-chatbots-are-not-the-future/). Points (from a model-assisted summary of the original page; the page showed no date): text boxes give no affordances; prompts are piled-up context that belongs in controls; responses are isolated so you scroll back and forth reading "line by line"; lack of a "working buffer" is "a complete non-starter"; implementing and evaluating are split, breaking flow; good tools make clear how they should and should not be used; she proposes persistent editable workspaces, context moved into controls, and tools over "operated machines" — [Wattenberger](https://wattenberger.com/thoughts/boo-chatbots). The page does not use the word "linear"; the linearity critique is implicit in the isolated-responses/working-buffer point.
- **Maggie Appleton, "Language Model Sketchbook, or Why I Hate Chatbots"** **[BL]**, ~2023. [Essay](https://maggieappleton.com/lm-sketchbook). Chat locks us into a "text-heavy, turn-based interface paradigm" that is "the lazy solution" and leaves most of the design space unexplored. Prototypes: Daemons (background personas in the editor), **Branches** (explore cause-and-effect chains, "Why did this happen?" / "What's likely to happen next?"), Epi (right-click reasoning assistant). The page does not criticize linearity explicitly; the objection is to turn-taking and open-endedness — [Essay](https://maggieappleton.com/lm-sketchbook).
- **Maggie Appleton, "Squish Meets Structure: Designing with Language Models"** talk **[BL]**, 2023. [Page](https://maggieappleton.com/squish-structure). Open-ended "Magic AI" inputs offload cognitive work to users, lack affordances, and hide reasoning; recommends small "spell-check sized" tools, prompt chaining, Wattenberger's style lenses, human-in-the-loop. Does not target linearity.
- **Geoffrey Litt, March 2023 blog post on LLMs and end-user programming** (URL slug `llm-end-user-programming`; I believe the title is "Malleable software in the age of LLMs", from memory, not on the fetched page) **[BL]**. [Post](https://www.geoffreylitt.com/2023/03/25/llm-end-user-programming). "Chat is an essentially limited interaction mode, regardless of the quality of the bot"; chat is slow, imprecise and requires conscious awareness of your thought process; "Chat will never feel like driving a car". Alternatives: a two-loop model (fast direct manipulation inner loop; LLM edits the software in an outer loop), spreadsheets as computational media, LLM-generated task-specific UI, malleable software.
- **Linus Lee, "Imagining better interfaces to language models"**, 1 May 2022 **[BL]**. [Post](https://thesephist.com/posts/latent/). "Conversations are a bad way to keep track of information" because users hold context in working memory; chat suits quick queries not multi-step work; prompting is "a single lever" with no direct manipulation of output. Proposes latent-space interfaces (drag a handle to shift tone, interpolate between two sentences, steer toward a destination concept). Does not discuss branching. Later work: Prism (Jun 2024), [post](https://thesephist.com/posts/prism/) (title only; not read). Lee also argued against chat-only on the Latent Space podcast ("Chat is NOT all you need") — [episode summary](https://www.latent.space/p/ai-interfaces-and-notion) (search snippet, not read in full).
- **Other critical writing (lower confidence, snippets only)**: UX Collective "Chat is the wrong interface for AI" argues chat presents communication as a chronological record — [link](https://uxdesign.cc/chat-is-the-wrong-interface-for-ai-de14d352de1b); "The chat box isn't a UI paradigm, it's what shipped" — [link](https://uxdesign.cc/the-chat-box-isnt-a-ui-paradigm-it-s-what-shipped-96e931d92769); Eric J. Ma, "Rethinking LLM interfaces: from chatbots to contextual applications" — [link](https://ericmjl.github.io/blog/2025/6/14/rethinking-llm-interfaces-from-chatbots-to-contextual-applications). I did not open these.
- **Linearity critiques stated in the HCI papers**: "[conversation's] inherent linear structure poses significant limitations for complex information tasks" (Sensecape) — [paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.11483); formative study found LLM interfaces give "long-winded responses" and users wanted to organize information across exchanges (Graphologue) — [paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.11473); linear chat forces users to "repeatedly compare and copy contextual information", raising cognitive load (Mindalogue) — [abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10570); chat prompts return "long, linear text" that makes backtracking hard (AI-Instruments) — [abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18736); Schon-based argument that reflective practice is non-linear and recursive while chat is linear (ChatGraPhT) — [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.22790).
- **Model-side evidence that long linear threads hurt**: Laban et al. found a ~39% average performance drop in multi-turn vs single-turn across six generation tasks, driven by increased unreliability; models "get lost" after a wrong turn and don't recover; appeared at ICLR 2026 — [arXiv 2505.06120](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.06120) (via [search summary](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.06120v1)). The Conversation Tree Architecture paper frames this as "logical context poisoning" — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21278). Contextify participants cited "long-conversation degradation" as a trigger for branching — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.07121).

### Inferences
- The essayists mostly argue for **structure, affordances and direct manipulation** rather than for branching specifically. Appleton's "Branches" is the closest, and is about causal-chain exploration. The HCI branching literature supplies the explicit "chat is linear" argument and the cognitive-load argument.
- Two distinct reasons recur for leaving linear chat: human-side (can't see, compare, reorganize, return) and model-side (long context degrades). A branching design addresses both only if the branch also *isolates context*; a pure visual tree over a shared context addresses only the first.

### Gaps
- Andy Matuschak: I found no primary Matuschak writing on LLM chat linearity (searched his notes site and general web) — [notes home](https://notes.andymatuschak.org/). Treat as unsupported.
- Bret Victor: no direct statement on LLM interfaces found. One Substack uses his "Inventing on Principle" talk to argue for feedback-rich LLM interfaces, and a Hacker News comment speculates about his view; neither is Victor — [search results](https://dspn.substack.com/p/rethinking-llm-interfaces-from-chatbots).
- Wattenberger's essay date: unconfirmed on the page; 15 May 2023 is the earliest dated reference I found.
- Litt's exact post title unverified on the fetched page.
- Lee's podcast and Prism pages were seen only as titles/snippets.

## 3. Recurring design patterns

### Takeaway
Seven patterns recur across the catalog: node = prompt + response; branch by replying to any node; ancestor-path-only context (with manual include/exclude); text-selection to spawn; semantic zoom / summary layers; dual chat + canvas views; and LLM-mediated merge (when present). "Foraging vs synthesizing" appears in Sensecape's design framing but not as an explicit mode switch elsewhere.

### Cited Findings
- **Node = prompt + response, reply on any node to branch**: ChatGPTree ([PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.22790)), Canvas Chat ([post](https://ericmjl.github.io/blog/2025/12/31/canvas-chat-a-visual-interface-for-thinking-with-llms/)), CanvasConvo ([HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.15848v2)), Contextify ([HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.07121)), Spellburst (duplicate operator; a menu operator must be chosen to extend a branch) ([PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.03921)), Loom ([README](https://github.com/socketteer/loom)), ChatGPT product ([TMCnet](https://blog.tmcnet.com/blog/rich-tehrani/?p=25101)).
- **Context inheritance along the ancestor path only**: ChatGPTree (root to node), Canvas Chat (all ancestors; union for multi-select), CanvasConvo (history up to branch point), Contextify (mainline to branch anchor, siblings isolated, manual Include/Exclude), Mindalogue (parent node only), VisCanvas (no chat history; goals along root-to-current path), Conversation Tree Architecture (isolated node windows; downstream flow on branch; upstream on delete), ChatGPT "Branch in new chat" (carry over up to chosen message). Sources as above plus [Mindalogue HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10570v1), [VisCanvas HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.21886), [arXiv 2603.21278](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21278).
- **Select text to spawn a branch / node**: Canvas Chat highlight-and-branch; Sensecape text extraction to nodes (avg 6.25 per session) ([paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.11483)); Graphologue click node/edge to issue follow-up prompts ([paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.11473)); CanvasConvo selection toolbar (shorten, expand, rephrase, summarize, critique) ([HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.15848v2)); Luminate highlighted text becomes focused context ([HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.12953)); DirectGPT localized prompting on a selection ([HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.03691v2)).
- **Summary nodes / semantic zoom**: Sensecape (Lines, Summary, Keywords), Luminate (five zoom levels), VisCanvas (three levels), Graphologue (per-paragraph summary and outline), CanvasConvo (auto-summarized branch zones and tags), Contextify (Memory Agent compresses between mainline and branches). Sources as above.
- **Foraging vs synthesizing**: Sensecape explicitly maps its canvas view (gather/organize) and hierarchy view (reason at higher abstraction) onto the foraging and sensemaking loops (Pirolli & Card), and lists "cost of context-switching between exploration and sensemaking" as a design challenge — [paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.11483). Luminate's "exploration" CSI factor scored highest — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.12953).
- **Dual view: linear chat + synchronized canvas/tree**: CanvasConvo, Contextify, Branchat. Finding: users selectively move between views and keep chat central — [abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15848). Sensecape authors also suggest offering a switch back to linear — [paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.11483).
- **Merging branch conclusions**: Spellburst (LLM merge plus diff); ChatGPTree/ChatGraPhT (multi-parent prompt node); Canvas Chat (multi-select synthesis); VisCanvas (Merge node); Graphologue (merge diagrams and nodes); Loom (merge node with parent/children, combine trees); Conversation Tree Architecture (volatile node selectively merged to parent or discarded); absent in CanvasConvo, Mindalogue, Contextify. Sources as above.
- **Auto-layout vs free canvas**: Spellburst chose auto-layout of the version-history tree to avoid cleanup work ([PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.03921)); a Sensecape participant said Miro boards "get very confusing pretty quickly" and liked one canvas per subtopic ([paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.11483)); CanvasConvo auto-lays out the conversation tree ([abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15848)).
- **Proactive AI suggestions for structure**: Contextify suggests branching, returning, pattern extraction (receptiveness varied with task phase) ([HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.07121)); Sensecape and Mindalogue generate subtopics/questions/explanations; Luminate generates dimensions; ChatGraPhT's Meta Agent gives reflective guidance.
- **Reified prompts/objects instead of transcript**: Cells/Generators/Lenses, DirectGPT, AI-Instruments, OOPrompt, Memolet. Sources as above.
- **Bookmarks / pinning**: Luminate (bookmarks and filters), Loom (bookmarks), Canvas Chat (pin matrix cells as nodes). I did not find a system whose explicit design element is "park a question for later".

### Inferences
- "Ancestor-path-only" is the de facto standard for what a branch knows, but systems differ on whether this is visible. CanvasConvo's finding that users were unsure what a branch "knew" suggests that making inherited context legible (Contextify's Include/Exclude toggles, Branchat's context scoping) is a requirement, not an extra.
- Merge is typically "select two or more nodes, ask the LLM to synthesize", which collapses the human sensemaking step into a generation call; no system I found offers a structured merge (for example, claim-level reconciliation).
- A consistent hybrid pattern is emerging: keep chat as the primary input, add a spatial/tree layer for overview and revisitation.

### Gaps
- No evidence on pinning/parking questions as a studied pattern.
- No comparison between "context-isolating" and "visual-only" branching.
- Whether summaries of branches faithfully represent branch content was not evaluated in any paper I read.

## 4. What the user studies actually show: branching/spatial vs linear

### Takeaway
Evidence is positive but thin and uneven. Gains appear on exploration breadth, structure, revisitation, and parallel exploration for open-ended tasks. Costs appear on learning curve, speed on simple or targeted tasks, and orientation in dense graphs. Cognitive-load findings are mixed: Branchat (preliminary, N=12) and MindTrellis report lower load; VisCanvas shows no difference; Contextify and Mindalogue participants report added overhead and disorientation. No study I found measured disorientation with a validated instrument.

### Cited Findings
Comparison table (evidence strength: N, baseline, stats):

| System (venue) | N | Baseline | Positive | Negative / null |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sensecape (UIST'23 PR) | 12 | Chat + basic concept-map canvas | more concepts (68.3 vs 22.8, p=.01), deeper hierarchy (4.3 vs 2.6, p=.02), more revisits (12.8 vs 0.7), 7/12 prefer overall | same prompts/nodes; baseline easier to navigate; hierarchy view overwhelming for some; learning curve — [paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.11483) |

- **Where chat holds up**: chat was better or preferred for linear step-by-step queries (node-tree study), for targeted single-chain analysis (VisCanvas), for short/simple tasks (Contextify), and for broad overviews (6 of 12 in Sensecape). Familiarity is cited repeatedly (Spellburst, VisCanvas, CanvasConvo, Threddy).
- **Where branching/spatial helps**: open-ended exploration and brainstorming (node-tree study, VisCanvas, Sensecape), revisiting earlier states and seeing structure (Sensecape, CanvasConvo), avoiding restarts (CanvasConvo participants, Spellburst P1/P9), parallel exploration (VisCanvas p=.01).
- **Orientation / complexity negatives**: Mindalogue ("easily lost direction"), Sensecape ("complex and overwhelming" for a few), Luminate (40 responses overwhelming), Graphologue ("a little intimidating"), VisCanvas (navigation costs may grow as graphs grow), CanvasConvo (unclear what a branch knows; view switching), Contextify (branching overhead; learning cost of node representation). All are self-report or authors' interpretation; none uses a disorientation measure.
- **Generative UI results** (non-branching alternative to chat): Stanford GenUI reports up to 72% human-preference gain over chat UIs but chat stayed competitive for simple/math queries (secondary summary) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.19227), [summary](https://aitopics.org/doc/arxivorg:C27E88F9); Google reports raters strongly preferred generative UI over standard LLM output when ignoring speed — [blog](https://research.google/blog/generative-ui-a-rich-custom-visual-interactive-user-experience-for-any-prompt/).
- Authors of several papers explicitly caution against generalization: Contextify (six exploratory participants), Sensecape (20-minute sessions, novelty effect), Luminate (25-minute task), Sood et al. (N=8), VisCanvas (homogeneous students, short sessions, possible TLX ceiling).

### Inferences
- The strongest design-relevant finding across studies is **task-type dependence**: structured/branching interfaces pay off for open-ended, divergent work and cost more for convergent, targeted work. This echoes the node-tree study's recommendation of adaptive interfaces that switch between structured and conversational forms, and CanvasConvo's observation that users use both views.
- Because the better-controlled comparisons (VisCanvas, Mindalogue) show null or negative results on speed and load, claims of reduced cognitive load rest on small or preliminary studies (Branchat, MindTrellis abstract) and should be treated cautiously.
- The only system-level study with strong objective gains versus a chat-like baseline (DirectGPT: speed, prompt counts) is about direct manipulation of the artifact, not branching. The branching literature mostly measures breadth, structure and perception, not output quality.

### Gaps
- No longitudinal study beyond CanvasConvo's five-day field deployment (which has no linear-chat comparison condition).
- No study of branch-context errors (for example wrong-branch answers), merge quality, or whether summary nodes lose information.
- No validated measure of orientation/disorientation applied to canvases; no test of scale (hundreds of nodes).
- Full texts for Branchat, MindTrellis, LLMartini and the Google/Stanford generative-UI papers were not read, so their effect sizes and baselines are unverified.
- Mindalogue's abstract-vs-body discrepancy on task steps is unresolved; v1 of the arXiv HTML was the only version read.
