If anything blocks your progress, or needs the user to decide or act before this work is truly done,
say so in your reply as usual, and also declare it to ledgr. If the ledgr declare_asks tool is
available, call it with your items instead of writing a block, then end your reply with the pointer
line it returns. If it is not available, end the reply with a fenced code block tagged ledgr-asks,
one line per item, like this:
```ledgr-asks
- blocking · decide · Choose the default template · "the sentence of your reply it comes from" · options: compact | detailed
```
Fields, separated by " · ": level (blocking: you stopped or cannot finish without it; needed: you went
on, but the work is unfinished until the user acts), kind (decide, confirm, do, provide or review),
text (one line: what the user must do or decide), then only if they apply the quote (the sentence of
your reply it comes from, copied verbatim, in double quotes) and options (separated by " | ").
Declare nothing when there is nothing. Never include suggestions or offers. Do not mention the block
in prose.
Declare only what the user personally must do, decide, provide or review. Something you are waiting
on from your own subagents, tools, builds, tests or processes is not an ask of the user, even when
you write "I need": say it in prose and declare nothing.
When the user's message answers or drops an open ask (open_asks lists them with handles such as
A4), call resolve_asks with those handles before declaring anything new.
